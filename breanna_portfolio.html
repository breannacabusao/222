Origin story · Fall 2025

Designing Third Spaces

"I was building this project in class when I found 222. It wasn't a coincidence — it was confirmation."

As PM, I led a team through the full HCD process: user interviews, ethnographic research, affinity mapping, lo-fi and hi-fi prototyping. The research question was simple — why do people feel lonely even when they're surrounded by people? The answer pointed directly to the collapse of third spaces. I was deep in that problem when 222 showed up in my feed. I've been paying attention ever since.

Always noticing

Serendipity is why I shoot on film.

I picked up a film camera because I wanted to slow down — to actually see what I was looking at before I pressed the shutter. That's what drew me to third spaces academically. And it's what drew me to 222. The small moment, the accidental conversation, the table that becomes a tradition — I've been documenting that before I had a name for it.

On recruiting

I've evaluated hundreds of people under pressure.

Greek recruitment at UC Berkeley runs in cycles — back-to-back evaluations, first impressions that count, a score card for every interaction, and a decision at the end that shapes the chapter for years. I led two full cycles (2024, 2025) as exec board lead. I know what it feels like to walk into a room of strangers, read the energy in thirty seconds, and know exactly who belongs.
"I haven't worked at Airbnb or Uber. What I have is a track record of walking into rooms full of strangers and leaving with exactly who we needed."
