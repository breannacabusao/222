Origin story

Designing Third Spaces

For a long time, I thought I had my future figured out: pre-med @ UC Berkeley, medical school, stable career. Instead, I found myself saying yes to everything. Research, jobs, club soccer, Greek life, a double major, and countless side quests. All trying to figure out what I actually cared about.

What kept showing up was community. Over time friends graduated, moved to new cities, and the more I paid attention, the more I became fascinated by third spaces and the role they play in helping people feel grounded, supported, and less alone.

I was exploring how to build something in this space myself. Then I found 222…

Always noticing

Serendipity is why I shoot on film.

What started with disposable cameras turned into an obsession after a stranger gifted me my first film camera. Film taught me to be present and appreciate the beauty of not knowing exactly how a moment will turn out. The photos I love most aren't of places. They're of the people: convos that last longer than expected, familiar faces that become friends, and the communities that form around them. Long before I knew what a "third space" was, I was documenting them.

On being human

You only live once. Am I right?

"Our 20-person team here dislikes the use of AI-generated content and is seriously concerned about the rise of slop online and the bastardization of 'creativity.' Every part of 222 has a human curation element. Every single question we've chosen to ask was hand written, obsessed over, and refined by members of the team."Keyan Kazemian, 222

Caught mid-run, mid-laugh, mid-everything · grad pics with Sydney

This resonates with me deeply.

In one of my favorite Berkeley classes, Neuropsychology of Happiness, we learned that the strongest predictor of happiness isn't money, status, or success. It's the quality of our relationships. Who we surround ourselves with.

When I look around me I see a world clawing for instant gratification. A world that is optimized for consumption with endless scrolling, with more time being spent on our devices than actually living it.

This is why I care about what 222 is building.

I don't want to spend my life optimizing feeds. I want to help create more IRL experiences for people to go outside, meet someone new, hear a story, and feel seen.

I mean, to me, that's work worth doing.
